War and Economic Struggles Deepen Gender Gaps in Israel and Egypt

Women in Israel and Egypt are facing increasing financial, social, and political setbacks, worsening gender disparities across multiple sectors. In his latest article for The Media Line, Nathan Klabin examines how war, economic hardship, and legal barriers are pushing women out of leadership roles and making daily survival more difficult.

The Impact of War on Gender Inequality in Israel

In Israel, the ongoing war has exacerbated gender inequality. Women make up only six out of 32 ministers in the government, and their representation in the Knesset has been steadily declining. The war's toll has extended beyond the battlefield, deepening economic struggles that affect women disproportionately.

The gender wage gap has worsened, now standing at a staggering 35%, which means that women in Israel earn only 65% of what their male counterparts do. Furthermore, budget cuts have led to the reduction of crucial programs such as childcare and vocational training, which many women rely on for both financial and personal stability.

Particularly affected are single mothers and caregivers, who are increasingly being forced out of the workforce. As Tali Nir, CEO of 121 – Engine for Social Change, points out, “This is an invisible crisis.” The growing burden on Israeli women is a social issue that is often overlooked in the broader conversation about the war's consequences.

Egypt's Gender Struggles Amid Economic Crisis

While the article primarily focuses on Israel, similar patterns are emerging in Egypt. As both nations grapple with economic hardships, the resulting gender inequality is becoming more pronounced. Women are being pushed further into economic precarity, with increasing unemployment rates and limited opportunities for career advancement.

In both countries, legal barriers and cultural norms contribute to the social challenges women face. In Egypt, these challenges are compounded by a lack of political representation and decision-making power, where women are underrepresented in government positions. This contributes to a cycle of inequality that becomes harder to break as economic conditions worsen.

Conclusion: A Call for Change

The situation in both Israel and Egypt highlights a growing crisis that demands urgent attention. Women, especially those in leadership roles or responsible for caregiving, are bearing the brunt of these socioeconomic challenges. If the current trends continue, the gender gap will only widen, making recovery from these crises more difficult for future generations.

As we look forward, it's crucial for policymakers and society at large to recognize the unique challenges that women face in these times of conflict and economic turmoil. By addressing these inequalities, there may be hope for a more balanced and fair recovery in the years to come.
